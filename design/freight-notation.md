# Freight Notation

A visual philosophy for plates that look like survey drawings from a working yard: things in motion, recorded with the patience of a cartographer.

## Field and ground

Every plate is a field of measured space. A faint grid lies under everything, fine as the ruling on surveyor's paper, so the eye senses order before it sees a single mark. Crop marks hold the corners. Nothing floats free: every shape sits on the grid, or deliberately breaks from it, and that break is the event the plate is about. Negative space is generous and exact. It is the quiet between shipments, measured to the pixel, and it is as considered as the marks themselves.

## Mark and repetition

Meaning builds through accumulation. A single mark says nothing; three hundred identical marks, one of them different, say everything. Plates are made from perfect primitives (the dot, the bar, the ring, the square, the short dash) repeated in rows, stacks, rings and lanes until they read like a census. The repetition must feel labored over, as if each element were placed by hand with a ruling pen over long evenings: equal intervals, consistent stroke weights, no accidental collisions. Master-level execution here means restraint. Add nothing that the pattern does not need.

## Color as signal

The palette is steel and paper with three signals. Neutrals carry the structure in several measured tints. Orange is the one thing that moved, failed or arrived. Teal is the system answering, a confirmation or a return path. Yellow is reserved for caution and appears rarely, in hazard bands no wider than a finger. Signals are scarce on purpose: a plate may hold hundreds of neutral marks and only one to five signal marks. The scarcity is what makes them legible, and it is calibrated with painstaking care so that both a dark and a light ground hold the same hierarchy.

## Scale and rhythm

Each plate pairs a dense field of small marks with one large, quiet gesture: an outline numeral, a long arc, a single heavy bar. Rhythm comes from intervals (fixed spacing, then one interval doubled) and from direction: lanes run left to right like a shift, stacks rise, rings close. The large gesture and the dense field never compete. One whispers at full scale; the other speaks in a multitude of small voices.

## Type as instrument

Type is an instrument reading, never a paragraph. A plate carries a plate number, a single noun, and a small caption in a clinical register, set in a narrow stencil face and a plain monospace at small sizes with open tracking. Labels sit at the edges like the stamped codes on a container door. They are aligned to the same grid as the marks and given the same care: every baseline measured, every margin equal, nothing touching anything it should not touch.

## Craft

The finished series should look like the product of deep expertise and countless hours: a coffee-table survey of an invisible discipline, documented as if it were observable fact. Each plate is distinct, yet all of them belong to one book through shared margins, shared grid, shared signal grammar. If a plate needs improving, refine what is there (an interval, a tint, a weight) rather than adding another shape.
